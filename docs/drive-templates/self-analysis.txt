HOW TO USE THIS

• Complete this independently before discussing answers with the other members.

• Write what is true now—not what sounds impressive or what you think the team wants to hear.

• Use the examples only to understand the expected depth. Do not copy them unless they are genuinely true for you.

• The final reflection should help you explain what self-fulfillment means to you in your own words.

 DO | What do I want to spend my life doing?

5. What do I want to spend more of my time doing?

 YOUR ANSWER

 [Write your answer here]​



 EXAMPLE

 Understanding real problems, designing solutions, building products or services, improving operations, making decisions, learning new

6. What do I want to spend less of my time doing?

 YOUR ANSWER

 [Write your answer here]​



 EXAMPLE

 Internal politics, repetitive reporting, unnecessary approval processes, or work where I have responsibility but little authority and

7. Why?

 YOUR ANSWER

 ENOUGH | What is enough for me?
 Define the point where more money, scale, or work would stop meaningfully improving your life.



18. At what point would additional income stop significantly improving my life?

 YOUR ANSWER
 [Write your answer here]​



 EXAMPLE

 If my personal income consistently reaches PHP 250,000–300,000 per month and I also have sufficient savings and investments,
 maximizing income beyond that would become less important than time, freedom, relationships, and meaningful work.




19. How large does a business need to become for me to feel successful?

 YOUR ANSWER

 [Write your answer here]​



 EXAMPLE

 I do not need to build a 1,000-person company. A profitable company with 20–50 strong employees, healthy cash flow, and the ability

20. Why is that enough?

 YOUR ANSWER

 [Write your answer here]​



 EXAMPLE

 I am more interested in building a durable and well-run business than pursuing size only for status. If the business can create good
 jobs, generate enough profit for the founders, and operate sustainably, I would consider that meaningful success.




21. How much of my time am I willing to give to the business?

 YOUR ANSWER

 [Write your answer here]​



 EXAMPLE

 During the first year I could accept 50–60 hour weeks for limited periods, but I would not want that to become permanent. Long term I
 would want the business to function with roughly 30–40 hours of my weekly involvement or less.




 NOT | What am I unwilling to sacrifice?
 Boundaries are part of the goal. Clarify what success is not allowed to cost you.
22. What am I not willing to sacrifice for the business?

 YOUR ANSWER

 [Write your answer here]​



 EXAMPLE

 My long-term health, important relationships, personal integrity, all of my financial safety, or all of my freedom for the sake of growth.

 YOUR ANSWER

 [Write your answer here]​



 EXAMPLE

 Building a highly profitable company but having no time outside work, constantly fighting with my co-founders, becoming dependent on
 practices I am not proud of, or being financially successful but unable to leave the business even for a short period.




24. What kind of business do I never want to run?

 YOUR ANSWER

 [Write your answer here]​



 EXAMPLE

 A business that depends on misleading customers, exploiting employees, hiding serious quality problems, or creating revenue through
 something I would be uncomfortable explaining openly to people I respect.

 YOUR ANSWER

 [Write your answer here]​



 EXAMPLE
